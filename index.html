About AllergyLocate

Zamin Rizvi of FARE's (Food Allergy and Research) Teen Advisory Group built this database because finding allergen information before eating somewhere shouldn't require 20 minutes of Googling. Most major restaurants already publish allergen PDFs or interactive tools, and we've now built the one place to access them all.

All links are verified manually by Zamin and a team of researchers.

AllergyLocate is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by any restaurant listed. We are an independent, referential directory. Allergen information is collected from publicly available sources and user submissions.

NJ Senate Bill S3394

The site exists to make allergen information more accessible online, but we can only make accessible what is available. NJ S3394 would mandate every restaurant to publish that information. With this database, research into how allergen information is made available online is streamlined.

The real gap? Independent restaurants. While we understand the limitations of small kitchens, the 33 million Americans managing food allergies deserve to be informed about whether or not the food they order contains something potentially deadly for them.

Coming soon: the Transparency Score

In development

Right now, AllergyLocate answers one question: does a restaurant publish allergen info at all? The Transparency Score will go a level deeper, rating how good that disclosure actually is.

Planned factors: accessibility (how many clicks?), currency (when was it last updated?), navigation (is it a ginormous PDF or an interactive search tool?), completeness (are all major allergens listed?), and awareness (cross-contact notification, allergy policy). A database like this shouldn't remain dormant. We're going to compare transparency across regions, types of restaurants, and price points.

The 9 major food allergens (US)

Per the FDA: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish, tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, and sesame. Restaurants in this database publish information about some or all of these. Most disclosures are at the category level — for example, "tree nuts" rather than specific nuts like hazelnut or almond. Always confirm your specific allergen with the restaurant directly.

Important limitations

  • Allergen information may be incomplete, outdated, or vary by location.
  • Cross-contact is always possible even when a dish is listed as allergen-free.
  • We link to publicly available information — we do not verify its medical accuracy.
  • This site does not provide medical advice. Always consult your doctor or allergist for guidance on managing your allergies.
  • Always confirm allergen information directly with the restaurant before ordering.
